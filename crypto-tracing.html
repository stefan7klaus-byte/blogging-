Following the Money After a Cryptocurrency Scam

A practical, lawful framework for organizing blockchain transaction information after suspected digital-asset fraud.

Cryptocurrency transactions are recorded on public blockchains, but a public ledger does not automatically reveal who controls every address.

1. Preserve the starting evidence

Record transaction hashes, addresses, asset, network, timestamp, amount and the source of each piece of information.

2. Build a transaction timeline

Follow confirmed transfers and record each hop, split, consolidation, bridge or service interaction.

3. Distinguish attribution from observation

An address receiving funds is an observable fact. Saying who controls it is an attribution claim requiring independent evidence.

4. Escalate appropriately

For significant losses, preserve evidence and consider qualified legal, financial or law-enforcement assistance. Do not attempt unauthorized access.
