What to Do in the First 24 Hours After a Cryptocurrency Scam

Zenith Hackers Intelligence · Educational guide

The first day after a suspected cryptocurrency scam can feel overwhelming. Prioritize protecting remaining accounts and preserving evidence. Fast, accurate reporting can help service providers assess the incident, but no response can guarantee funds will be returned.

1. Secure your accounts and devices

  • From a trusted device, change passwords for affected email, exchange and related accounts; enable strong multifactor authentication where available.
  • If a wallet seed phrase or private key may have been exposed, treat that wallet as compromised. Do not reuse the phrase or enter it into a website suggested by a stranger.
  • Revoke suspicious connected-app permissions where appropriate, and contact the relevant wallet or platform provider using independently verified support channels.

2. Preserve the evidence

  • Record transaction hashes, wallet addresses, blockchain network, asset, amount, date and time, and any destination addresses shown by the transaction explorer.
  • Save chats, emails, phone numbers, websites, social profiles, invoices and payment instructions. Keep original files and note the timeline while details are fresh.
  • Do not delete messages or reset a device before considering whether records may be needed for an investigation.

3. Notify the relevant services

  • Contact the exchange, bank, payment provider or wallet service involved through its official website or app. Ask for a case number and provide concise evidence.
  • Report the scam to the appropriate law-enforcement or cybercrime reporting body in your country. If an exchange appears to be the destination, provide the transaction trail through its official reporting channel.

4. Watch for follow-up fraud

  • Scammers often contact victims again posing as recovery agents, lawyers, regulators or investigators. Be suspicious of unsolicited messages and guaranteed outcomes.
  • Do not pay advance release fees, taxes or unlock charges to a person who promises to return crypto. Verify credentials and engagement terms independently.

Important: No asset recovery outcome is guaranteed. Never share wallet seed phrases, private keys, passwords or one-time codes. This article is general educational information, not legal, financial or law-enforcement advice.
