CASE STUDY · DIGITAL ASSETS

Following the Money After a Cryptocurrency Scam

A practical, lawful framework for organizing blockchain transaction information after a suspected digital-asset fraud.

Cryptocurrency transactions are recorded on public blockchains, but a public ledger does not automatically reveal who controls every address. Good investigation starts by separating what the ledger proves from what still requires corroboration.

1. Preserve the starting evidence

Record the transaction hash, sending and receiving addresses, asset, network, timestamp, amount and the source of each piece of information. Preserve screenshots and original communications separately from your analytical notes.

2. Build a transaction timeline

Follow confirmed transfers from the known starting transaction. Record each hop, noting when funds split, consolidate, bridge between networks or reach a service that may have additional records.

3. Distinguish attribution from observation

An address receiving funds is an observable fact. Saying that a particular person controls that address is an attribution claim that requires independent evidence. Avoid presenting labels, guesses or third-party claims as proof.

4. Escalate appropriately

When a loss is significant, preserve the evidence and consider qualified legal, financial or law-enforcement assistance. Do not attempt unauthorized access to accounts, private systems or exchange records.
