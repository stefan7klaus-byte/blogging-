Blockchain Confirmations and Why They Matter

Zenith Hackers Intelligence · Educational guide

1. Start with verifiable information

A confirmation indicates that a transaction has been included in a block and has received subsequent network validation. The meaning of a confirmation count differs by network and service policy.

2. Document the process

Record the displayed status and time checked, and follow the relevant network's conventions. If a service has its own deposit threshold, distinguish that service policy from the blockchain's status.

3. Understand the limitations

Confirmation does not make a transaction reversible or guarantee a service will credit a deposit. For important decisions, recheck the transaction and consult the relevant provider's official guidance.

Practical checklist

  • Record dates and time zones consistently.
  • Keep transaction hashes, addresses, receipts and messages together.
  • Save source links and preserve original records.
  • Use verified official reporting channels.
  • Never share seed phrases, private keys, passwords or one-time codes.
